# Business Model And Pricing

Issue: #35  
Structured file: `pilot/research/business_positioning.json`

## Pricing Hypothesis

Initial buyer: **small to mid-sized Swiss architecture office**.

Initial model: **office subscription + active project packs**.

| Product step | Buyer | Pricing unit | Value driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVP1/MVP2 regulatory + permit | Architect office | Seat + active project pack | Faster parcel/permit orientation; fewer unpaid loops. |
| MVP3 fees/cost/tender | Architect office or economist partner | Cost/tender module | Fee erosion visibility; assumptions/exclusions control. |
| MVP4 memory/site/handover | Office or owner project team | Project memory module | Traceable decisions, defects and handover evidence. |

Later options:

- insurer/liability partner subsidy around the traceability ledger;
- enterprise owner project memory;
- jurisdiction-pack maintenance subscription.

## Why Not Per-Question Pricing

The value is not a single answer. It is continuity across a mandate: sources, decisions, risks, permit evidence,
tender assumptions, site decisions and handover memory. Pricing should follow active projects and modules, not
isolated prompts.

## First Monetizable Wedge

MVP1/MVP2 is the first paid wedge because it works without BIM and targets high-friction work that small offices
already do: feasibility, dossier preparation, and evidence-backed permit readiness.
