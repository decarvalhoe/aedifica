# The Architect's Reality — Holistic, Swiss, Intake to Handover

> Purpose: anchor Aedifica in what a Swiss architect *actually does and is liable for*, across the full
> mandate — not the "architect-draftsman" caricature. Everything here is a corrective to a framing that
> centers the BIM model. Phase codes, norm numbers and fee mechanics are verified against SIA / CRB /
> official sources (see end).

## 1. The architect is three roles, not a drawing tool

`prSIA 102` art. 1.1 defines the architect as providing **intellectual services over the whole life-cycle
of the building**. Three functions are bundled by default and **can be mandated separately** — which
matters because whoever holds a function holds its risk:

- **Direction générale du projet (DGP / _Gesamtleitung_)** — creative, technical *and* organisational
  lead; "coordinates, in space and time, all specialist planning and all involved specialists";
  owner of the **quality / cost / deadline** triangle.
- **Planification spécialisée (_Fachplanung_)** — the architectural design proper (concept, plans, models, dossiers).
- **Direction des travaux (DT / _Bauleitung_)** — represents the client toward contractors, directs and
  supervises the site. **Often a separate mandate** — and the one carrying the most site risk.

**Drawing/modeling is a fraction of one of these three roles.** A product that automates drawing addresses
a sliver of the job.

## 2. The architect is the coordinator of 6–10 liable disciplines

In DGP, the architect orchestrates specialists, each governed by **their own SIA fee règlement** (own
contract, own fees, own liability):

| Specialist | Domain | Règlement |
|---|---|---|
| Ingénieur civil | Structure / statics | SIA 103 |
| Ingénieurs CVCR (HLKKS) | Heating, ventilation, A/C, refrigeration | SIA 108 |
| Ingénieur sanitaire / électricien | Fluids / power | SIA 108 |
| Architecte-paysagiste | Outdoor works | SIA 105 |
| Géomètre (officiel) | Survey, setting-out | ad hoc |
| Physicien du bâtiment | Thermal / acoustic / moisture | SIA 108 / ad hoc |
| Géotechnicien | Soil, foundations | ad hoc |
| Spécialiste sécurité incendie | AEAI concept | AEAI-approved |
| Acousticien | SIA 181 | ad hoc |

> **The product surface is here:** not drawing, but *framing the interfaces of responsibility* between
> disciplines — who owes what, when, on what basis. Coordination is where the architect's liability lives
> (see §6).

## 3. The corrected SIA phase model (this is the spine, and the current docs get the codes wrong)

Two coexisting norms: **SIA 112:2014** (the shared *understanding* model — phases & sub-phases for all
actors) and **SIA 102** (the *contractual* règlement — ordinary services & fees per sub-phase).

| Phase | Sub-phases (official codes) |
|---|---|
| **1 Définition des objectifs** | **11** Énoncé des besoins, approche méthodologique |
| **2 Études préliminaires** | **21** Définition du projet, étude de faisabilité · **22** Procédure de choix des mandataires _(← concours / SIA 142–143 live here)_ |
| **3 Étude du projet** | **31** Avant-projet · **32** Projet de l'ouvrage · **33** Procédure de demande d'autorisation / mise à l'enquête |
| **4 Appel d'offres** | **41** Appels d'offres, comparaison des offres, proposition d'adjudication |
| **5 Réalisation** | **51** Projet d'exécution · **52** Exécution de l'ouvrage · **53** Mise en service, achèvement |
| **6 Exploitation** | **61** Fonctionnement · **62** Surveillance / contrôle / entretien · **63** Maintenance |

**Corrections vs the current foundation docs** (which use "31 preliminary project", "3.2 Project", split
phase 4 into three, etc.):

- Feasibility (_étude de faisabilité_) is **21**, not phase 1.
- Competition / mandate selection (_concours_) is **22** — a sub-phase the docs omit entirely.
- Phase 4 is a **single code 41**, not three.
- Phase 6 has **three** sub-phases (61/62/63), and **63 Maintenance** is distinct.

> **Forward-compat caveat — SIA 102:2024 (ed. 2026 "harmonised RPH", currently in consultation):** adds a
> **Phase 0 "Initialisation"** and splits phase 4 into **41 "Planification de l'appel d'offres" + 42
> "Appel d'offres…adjudication"**. Aedifica must model **both** referentials, because live contracts will
> use the older one for years. (The draft itself carries a "no validity / do not use as reference" notice
> — confirm against the paid SIA 102:2014/2020 before any contractual use.)

## 4. Value and risk concentrate downstream — phases 32 / 41 / 52

Indicative ordinary-fee weighting (SIA 102:2014/2020 practice; phases 3–5 sum to 100 %):

| Sub-phase | Service | ~% of fee |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | Avant-projet | ~9 % |
| **32** | **Projet de l'ouvrage** | **~21 %** |
| 33 | Autorisation / mise à l'enquête | ~2.5 % |
| **41** | **Appel d'offres, comparaison, adjudication** | **~18 %** |
| 51 | Projet d'exécution | ~16 % |
| **52** | **Exécution (direction des travaux)** | **~29 %** |
| 53 | Mise en service, achèvement | ~4.5 % |

**Implication:** the value — and the work an agent can most leverage — sits in **project development (32),
adjudication (41) and execution (52)**: coordination, tenders, site. *Not* in producing 3D geometry, and
*not* in phase 33 (worth ~2.5 % of fees, yet the highest *risk* — see §5–6).

## 5. Compliance is a legal gate at phase 33 — and fire law is binding, BIM is not

Phase **33 (authorization / mise à l'enquête)** is the regulatory checkpoint. The authority *requires*
justifications, and the binding technical layer is **energy + fire + accessibility + seismic**, not BIM:

| Norm | Object | Binding nature |
|---|---|---|
| **SIA 380/1** | Thermal energy / heating demand | Required justification at 33 (via cantonal energy law) |
| **SIA 500** | Constructions sans obstacles (accessibility) | Execution rule of federal **LHand** — binding once a permit is filed |
| **SIA 261** | Actions on structures (incl. **seismic**, 5 zones Z1a–Z3b) | Structural dimensioning at 32–33 |
| **SIA 181** | Building acoustics | 31–32, checked at 53 |
| **AEAI / VKF fire prescriptions (2015; 2026 revision)** | Fire protection | **Mandatory cantonal law** via the AIET concordat — *not* optional |
| **SIA 118** | General conditions for execution (contract) | Governs 41–53 once referenced in the works contract |

> **Two corrections for the vision:**
> 1. **AEAI fire prescriptions are binding cantonal law**, unlike SIA norms (private-law, apply only if
>    contractually referenced). The compliance engine must treat AEAI differently from SIA norms.
> 2. **BIM is contractual, not normative.** **SIA 2051 is a *cahier technique*, recently withdrawn / under
>    revision** — BIM lives in the project convention (SIA 102 art. 2.7 "gestion numérique de l'information"),
>    subordinate to the phases and the contract. Framing BIM as a Swiss standard/expectation will lose a
>    Swiss architect's trust on contact.

## 6. The economic and liability reality a "metadata" framing misses

**Fee erosion is existential.**
- Legacy SIA 102 ties fees to the **coût de l'ouvrage déterminant** — so designing *cheaper* mechanically
  *reduces* the fee while the intellectual effort is unchanged. Economical design is punished.
- SIA 102:2024 shifts to **H = T × h** (time × average hourly rate) + statistics. The agentic lever is
  therefore to **reduce ordinary-service time** and, crucially, to **document and bill the _prestations
  spéciales_** (durability/labels, BIM, staged execution, circular economy) that art. 7.2 lists as
  "to be agreed specifically" — i.e. unpaid unless explicitly claimed. Today architects absorb them.
- Down-payments are capped (~90 %), so cash-flow tension is structural; modifications are billable *in
  régie* but **must be actively claimed**.

**Three cost taxonomies, orthogonal and all necessary** (all CRB standards):

```
eCCC-Bât / eBKP-H        →   NPK / CAN                →   CFC / BKP
(by element: design &        (by standardized article:    (by trade: adjudicate,
 steer cost early)            write comparable tenders)     invoice, finance)
```

The **mapping eCCC-Bât ↔ NPK ↔ CFC** (exchanged as `.crbx` / IfA18) is itself a high-value asset; the
model is only its support. (See competitive note in [`challenge-and-brainstorm.md`](challenge-and-brainstorm.md).)

**Liability is the real risk center.**
- The architect is a **fiduciary** ("personne de confiance", independent of contractors/suppliers); duties
  include loyalty, diligence/règles de l'art, spontaneous information, advice, and **duty to warn**.
- `prSIA 102` art. 8.6: the architect answers for **"défauts de coordination ou de surveillance"**,
  missed deadlines, **insufficient cost information**. **Failed coordination is a liability-engaging fault.**
- Defect/garantie clocks: **denunciation 60 days**; for the architect's services, **prescription 5 years**
  from acceptance; for the *built work* (SIA 118), a **2-year warranty** then **5-year prescription**.
- The architect is **not** guarantor of the *building* (the contractor is, via SIA 118) but **is** guarantor
  of *their own service* — conception, coordination, supervision, cost information.

> **The single most important product implication:** automating coordination **shifts liability**. The
> defensible value of Aedifica is therefore **decisional and contractual traceability** — who decided what,
> when, on what sourced basis, with what approval — far more than completeness of object metadata.

## Implications for Aedifica (carried into the challenge doc)

1. Organise around **SIA sub-phases and their fee weighting**, not around model levels of detail (LOD/LOIN).
2. Lead where money and risk are: **32 / 41 / 52 + the 33 compliance gate**.
3. Treat **conformity as time-stamped blocking checks** (energy / AEAI / SIA 500 / seismic), distinguishing
   binding AEAI/cantonal law from contractual SIA norms.
4. Make the **cost-taxonomy bridge** (eCCC ↔ NPK ↔ CFC, `.crbx`) a first-class capability.
5. Make **decisional/contractual traceability** a core, not a feature — it is what protects the liable architect.
6. Do **not** present BIM as a Swiss norm; treat it as an optional project convention.
